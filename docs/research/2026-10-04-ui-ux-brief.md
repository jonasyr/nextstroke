# Clear the chrome so the drawing leads

NextStroke's compare screen should look like a leading iPhone photo editor, not a form. The two photos fill the screen. A thin top bar holds only back, undo/redo and one export action. One bottom panel shows the controls for the active mode (Vergleich, Ausrichten, Ecken) and nothing else. Colour is used only for the active state and for at most one primary action per view. Every gesture (pinch, drag, hold, corner drag) gets a visible single-pointer alternative, which WCAG 2.2 requires anyway. On the icons-vs-labels conflict the rule is split. Icon-only (with a German accessible name) is used for the handful of platform-standard actions people already know: back, undo, redo, share, more, zoom. Icon plus short label, or text alone, is used for everything NextStroke invents: overlay vs split, the reference layer, paper corners, auto-detect. This matters because the owner's complaints (coloured text buttons everywhere, a stacked text navbar, equal-weight controls) break explicit Apple rules: one or two prominent buttons per view, monochrome controls over colourful content, at most three toolbar groups. The fix is mostly subtraction plus a clear mode structure. For the stack, use Lucide icons (ISC) with one custom corner-pin glyph and Base UI (MIT) for the slider, segmented control, toolbar and sheet. Both are compatible with AGPL and the repository's license allowlist. Base UI replaces the Radix line in D-036, so the switch needs a decision-log entry. The brief closes with a screen-by-screen layout, a full control inventory with German strings, and a three-round beginner usability test on a real iPhone.

## Every leading editor uses the same skeleton

The surveyed iPhone editors converge on one layout. In Apple Photos, "the Cancel button is in the top-left corner of the screen and the Done button is in the top-right corner," the photo sits in the centre, and a single row of tool buttons (Portrait, Adjust, Filters, Crop, Clean Up) runs along the bottom ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/guide/iphone/iphb08064d57/ios)). Choosing Adjust swaps that row for a horizontally scrolling row of round parameter icons plus **one slider for whichever parameter is selected**. A ring around each icon shows how far that setting has moved from default (same source). Lightroom mobile shows the precise value "at the top of the screen" while the slider moves ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/ar/learn/lightroom-cc/web/lightroom-mobile-adjustments)). Photomator reduces the whole iPhone toolbar to **six controls**: Cancel, Undo (hold for Redo or Revert to Original), Compare, the tools, More, and Done ([Pixelmator](https://support.pixelmator.com/photomator-user-guide/get-started/interface-overview)). Darkroom goes furthest. The photo runs edge to edge behind overlaid top actions, secondary items sit in a "•••" menu, and "a single brief tap clears every control so you see only the photo" ([Darkroom](https://darkroom.co/help/guide/interface-overview)). Snapseed's June 2025 redesign moved in the same direction. It now has three bottom tabs, tools grouped into four categories, export at the top right, and **a visible arc controller replacing the invisible horizontal swipe** ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/06/12/snapseed-3-0-update-iphone/)). Lightroom regrouped its tools into bundles to cut lateral scrolling and gesture complexity ([PopPhoto](https://www.popphoto.com/adobe-lightroom-mobile-for-ios-gets-new-interface-desktop-version-gets-new-features-and-performance)). None of these apps puts several equal-weight sliders or a wall of labelled buttons on the main screen. The current NextStroke UI is the outlier, not the norm.

Two lessons from this group carry over directly. First, **state must be legible without relying on colour**. Halide uses yellow only for the active state, yet a tester shot an entire trip without RAW "because I thought the deactivated state was the opposite," and the team redesigned the toggle as a result ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=x6bv1a36)). NextStroke toggles therefore change shape or fill (outline vs filled eye, a checked segment) as well as colour. Second, **thin controls are a regression**. MacStories found the iOS 18 Photos video scrubber, shrunk to a thin bar, harder to grab than the old one ([MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-18-the-macstories-review/9)). The opacity slider and the split divider need large grab areas. Halide's framing fits the beginner audience: the stock camera is "a little too simple, while advanced apps feel like an airplane cockpit" ([Lux](https://www.lux.camera/introducing-halide/)). NextStroke should sit between the two, with good defaults visible and precision one level down.

### Document scanners already solved the four-corner problem

The corner flow in D-054 is a document-scanner interaction, and scanners have converged here too. Apple Notes asks the user to "adjust the corners of the scan by dragging, then tap Keep Scan," and the community tip is to "tap a bit away from the magnifying glass in each corner and then drag." That tip only works because the drag is relative ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2020/08/21/ios-how-to-scan-documents-notes-app/)). WeScan's source code makes the mechanics explicit ([WeScan ZoomGestureController](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WeTransfer/WeScan/master/Sources/WeScan/Edit/ZoomGestureController.swift)). A press with zero delay selects **the corner closest to the first touch**. The corner then moves by the finger's **delta**, so it never jumps under the fingertip. A highlighted corner grows from 20 pt to a 75 pt circle showing a **2.5× zoom**, and points are clamped to the image with no snapping. When detection fails, WeScan places a default quad **inset 75 pt** from the image edges ([WeScan EditImageViewController](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WeTransfer/WeScan/master/Sources/WeScan/Edit/EditImageViewController.swift)).

Genius Scan pairs a magnifier ("without your fingers obstructing the preview") with two escape buttons: **Detect**, which re-runs detection, and **Maximize**, which reverts to the full photo ([Genius Scan](https://help.geniusscan.com/document-management/viewing-and-editing-documents/edit-crop-and-rotate-a-scan)). Adobe Scan offers Auto-detect and No Crop, and it falls back with "No document found. Capture manually" ([Adobe Scan docs](https://www.adobe.com/devnet-docs/adobescan/android/en/scan.html)). Google's ML Kit flow also shows a magnifier while corners are adjusted ([ML Kit](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/doc-scanner)). In-place loupes have a known weakness, though. One Adobe Scan user needed "10 times each until I get lucky" on a small screen ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-517/need-higher-precision-cropping-aid-11297)). The research-grade answer is Vogel and Baudisch's **Shift**: a **26 mm circular callout placed 22 mm above the touch**, with a crosshair, that flips position near screen edges. It cut errors on small targets ([Vogel & Baudisch, CHI 2007](https://projects.csail.mit.edu/hci-reading/papers/chi2007-vogel.pdf)). D-054 already specifies a 3× magnifier above the finger with a grab offset. The evidence supports that spec and adds three refinements: nearest-corner selection, a crosshair, and an edge flip. No surveyed art app offers four-corner rectification. Da Vinci Eye's AR mode instead needs a playing card and a ruler for anchoring ([The Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2023/08/26/da-vinci-eye-review-record-your-art-the-easy-way/)). This flow is NextStroke's differentiator and deserves the most polish.

### Onion skin should be the default compare mode, with split second

GitHub's image diff names three modes and gives each a job. **2-up** shows overall size and composition, **swipe** shows local pixel and colour differences, and **onion skin** finds elements "shifted by small amounts" ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/working-with-files/using-files/working-with-non-code-files)). A beginner's typical error is a contour placed a few millimetres off, which is a positional shift. That makes the opacity overlay, plus a hold-to-blink, the main tool. Split view is better for judging line weight or tone on either side of a feature. Hold-to-reveal is the dominant photo-editor convention. Lightroom shows "Before" while you hold ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/ar/learn/lightroom-cc/web/lightroom-mobile-adjustments)), and Darkroom calls hold-to-compare "the single most useful habit in the app" ([Darkroom](https://darkroom.co/help/guide/interface-overview)). Apple Photos instead toggles on a tap ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/guide/iphone/iphb08064d57/ios)). Photomator's compare is a split preview with a draggable divider ([Pixelmator](https://support.pixelmator.com/photomator-user-guide/get-started/interface-overview)). Practitioner guidance for before/after sliders asks that each side be labelled and that both images share the same frame ([Calgary design system](https://summit.calgary.ca/components/media-overview/before-and-after-slider.html)). The second point is exactly what NextStroke's alignment provides. The research found no controlled study comparing these modes, so "onion skin first" is a reasoned default. The usability rounds should confirm it.

### Art-helper apps show what beginners expect, and what makes them angry

The category splits three ways: camera-overlay tracers (Da Vinci Eye, SketchAR), lightbox tracers, and reference analysers. NextStroke belongs to the third group, next to **Drawing Proportions** (3.7/5 from 68 ratings), which aligns drawing and reference automatically and offers split view ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/drawing-proportions/1490326761)). It also sits next to **Overlay – Figure Drawing Tool**, which has too few ratings to display ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/py/app/overlay-figure-drawing-tool/id1275927763)). The expected vocabulary is opacity, lock, flip, grid, zoom and line/value filters. Da Vinci Eye, the leader at 4.5/5 from 12,000+ ratings, bundles these with a guided setup tutorial ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/da-vinci-eye-ar-trace-draw/id1120304868)). Complaints cluster around live-AR flicker ("images I'm using are just constantly flickering"; [grand-screen](https://grand-screen.com/apps/ar-art-projector-da-vinci-eye/reviews/)), holding the phone while drawing, right-hand bias in SketchAR ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/sketchar/reviews)), and ads after every action. TraceArt sits at 2.1/5, and one reviewer also called it "not very easy or intuitive in switching from one photo to another" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1539017013)). NextStroke compares a still photo after rectifying it, so it avoids the flicker complaint. It runs offline with no account, so it avoids the paywall complaint. The image-switching complaint is a warning: changing the Vorlage or the Zeichnung must take one tap from the editor. One more lesson: "lock" means two things in this category, freezing the overlay's transform and blocking touches on the screen. If NextStroke ever adds either, they need different names.

## Icons where iOS has taught them, words where NextStroke invents

The sources genuinely conflict here, so the rule has to be chosen deliberately. NN/g's position is that only a few icons are universally understood (home, print, search). "Most icons continue to be ambiguous," and "icon labels should be visible at all times." It adds the 5-second test: if you cannot think of an icon for a concept in five seconds, an icon will not communicate it ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/)). Apple's toolbar guidance goes the other way: "Prefer simple, recognizable symbols for items instead of text, except for actions like edit that aren't well-represented by symbols." It also says to use the standard Back and Close symbols and never a text label that says Back or Close ([Apple HIG, Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)). The two positions are less opposed than they look. Apple's own caveat ("Don't make people guess or experiment") is NN/g's point. NN/g's evidence concerns unfamiliar icons, and Apple's preference concerns standard ones. Learned icons do get found faster than text, roughly half the search time in a 2002 study, but "visual recognition is useless if users don't know what the icons mean" ([UX Movement](https://uxmovement.com/mobile/why-you-shouldnt-always-label-your-icons/)). Touch also rules out the usual escape hatch: tooltips never appear on touch, so hints cannot depend on them ([React Aria](https://react-aria.adobe.com/Tooltip)).

**The rule chosen for NextStroke has three tiers.**

1. **Icon-only, with a German accessible name.** Use this only for symbols iOS itself has taught (back chevron, undo, redo, share, more "…", zoom +/−, the close ×) and for direct-manipulation handles.
2. **Icon plus a one-word label.** Use this for the mode tabs in the bottom panel, the eye toggle, and any action that is either app-specific or rarely used.
3. **Text alone.** Use this for commit and abandon actions ("Fertig", "Abbrechen", "Weiter"), for the overlay/split segmented control (Apple: "Prefer using either text or images — not a mix of both — in a single segmented control"; [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/segmented-controls)), and for reset actions.

Text actions sit in their own group, apart from symbol buttons, because Apple warns that a text action next to a symbol "can create the illusion of a single action" (Toolbars). German compounds run long, so labels are capped at about 12 characters. Where no short word works, the control becomes an icon with an accessible name and moves into a level-2 panel, not onto a longer button. Apple also notes that symbols placed right next to the object they affect need less explanation ([Apple HIG, Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons)), which is why the corner tools sit next to the selected corner. The beginner tests decide whether any tier-1 icon moves to tier 2.

### One prominent action, monochrome everything else

Apple caps prominent buttons at "one or two per view," asks for "style — not size" to mark the preferred choice, and for toolbars says to "aim for a maximum of three" groups. It also says to "prefer using the default monochromatic appearance" when "your app already has bright, colorful content" ([Apple HIG, Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons); [Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)). A drawing photo with a coloured reference is exactly that kind of content. Progressive disclosure helps learnability, efficiency and error rates, but "designs that go beyond 2 disclosure levels typically have low usability" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). Hick's law argues for fewer simultaneous choices, with the explicit caveat not to "simplify to the point of abstraction" ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/)). It does not justify hiding the opacity slider, which beginners use constantly. NextStroke therefore uses two levels only. Level 1 is the canvas, the top bar, the mode tabs, and the active mode's single primary control. Level 2 is the "Feinjustierung" row inside a mode and the "Mehr" menu. Nothing sits at level 3.

## Gestures speed things up; visible controls guarantee access

Direct manipulation (pinch, drag, rotate the layer itself) is the right primary model for alignment because each action is "physical, incremental, and reversible" with immediate feedback. NN/g lists its costs: poor discoverability, poor precision, and exclusion of motor-impaired users. It recommends "alternatives such as numeric input and explicit commands" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/direct-manipulation/)). WCAG 2.2 makes this mandatory. **SC 2.5.1 (Level A)** requires every multipoint gesture, pinch zoom included, to work with a single pointer, and its map example adds +/− buttons ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures.html)). **SC 2.5.7 (AA)** requires every dragging operation to work without dragging. A slider whose track can be tapped qualifies ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)). Apple says the same in platform terms: "Offer alternatives to gestures," and add custom gestures only "in a game or drawing app," as shortcuts that supplement standard gestures rather than replace them ([Apple HIG, Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility); [Gestures](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gestures)). In NextStroke, then, pinch zoom gets a zoom capsule. Layer drag, scale and rotate get nudge arrows and ± steppers. Corner drag gets "select corner + arrows". Hold-to-reveal gets an eye toggle. The split divider and opacity get tappable slider tracks. The alternatives also solve precision: one tap on an arrow moves a corner by one screen pixel, which a fingertip cannot do.

Gesture conflicts need one meaning per gesture in each mode. In Vergleich mode, one-finger drag pans the view and pinch zooms it. In Ausrichten mode, the same gestures move and scale the Vorlage layer. That matches the legacy behaviour ("two-finger pan/zoom versus reference transform in align mode") and a visible mode chip makes it explicit. Apple Photos uses tap-to-toggle-original and Darkroom uses tap-to-hide-chrome, so a single tap cannot mean both. NextStroke keeps the legacy meaning: **tap on the canvas toggles "nur Zeichnung"**, and hold peeks. Hiding the chrome becomes an explicit button. Apple requires that hidden toolbars can be "reliably restore[d]" (Toolbars), and accidental chrome loss would strand beginners.

### Teach one gesture at the moment it is needed

Upfront tutorials do not help. In NN/g's 70-user iPhone study, success was 91% with the tutorial and 94% without (not significant), and **people who read the tutorial rated the tasks significantly harder** (4.92 vs 5.49 of 7) ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/)). Hint screens shown in a barrage get dismissed faster. Hints work best one at a time, focused on "a single interaction," and visually distinct from the real UI ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-instructional-overlay/)). NextStroke therefore has no intro carousel. Each mode shows one dismissible hint strip the first time it is entered ("Halten = nur Zeichnung", "Ecke ziehen oder antippen und mit Pfeilen bewegen"), stored per device, plus a "Gesten" reference under Mehr.

### Reach favours a bottom panel, but not the folk heat map

Hoober's field data covers 1,333 observations. While touching the screen, **49% held the phone one-handed, 36% cradled it, and 15% used both hands**, and people switched grip "sometimes every few seconds" ([UXmatters 2013](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php)). People prefer to look at and touch the centre. Targets need about 7 mm in the centre but about 12 mm in corners. Hoober calls the popular thumb-sweep chart "incorrect" and recommends primary content in the centre, secondary actions along the top and bottom edges, and tertiary actions in corners ([UXmatters 2017](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2017/03/design-for-fingers-touch-and-people-part-1.php)). That is exactly the editor skeleton: canvas in the centre, mode controls in a bottom panel, rare actions in a top-corner menu. For tool options that act on the content behind them, Apple sanctions a **nonmodal sheet** whose medium detent is "about half" the screen, with a grabber that "also works with VoiceOver." It also says to show only one sheet at a time, and that complex flows such as photo editing deserve a full-screen modal ([Apple HIG, Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)). NextStroke's editor is that full-screen modal, and its bottom panel behaves like a fixed-height nonmodal sheet that never covers more than about 35% of the screen. Commit placement involves a second conflict. Apple puts Cancel leading and Done trailing **in the top toolbar of a sheet** (Sheets), while Snapseed commits inside the tool. NextStroke puts "Abbrechen" and "Fertig" in the **header row of the bottom panel**. That is the panel's own top bar, so it follows Apple's rule, and it stays within thumb reach.

## Accessibility sets the numeric floor

Apple requires **44×44 pt hit regions**. WCAG 2.2 AA (SC 2.5.8) requires 24×24 CSS px, but W3C recommends the 44 px AAA target for important controls ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons); [W3C 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). NextStroke uses 44 px everywhere and 48 px at screen corners, following Hoober's larger corner targets. Icons and state indicators need **3:1 contrast** against adjacent colours ([W3C 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)), and text needs 4.5:1. Controls float over arbitrary photos, so contrast can only be guaranteed by placing them on a solid or blurred backing plate with a known colour. Every icon-only control needs a programmatic name. Where a label is visible, the accessible name must contain it (WCAG 4.1.2 and 2.5.3, [W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/label-in-name.html)), and toggles must expose `aria-pressed` or `aria-checked`. Apple asks apps to support text enlarged to at least 200% and to honour Reduce Motion for automatic zooming and scaling. Motion that tracks a finger directly is fine ([Apple HIG, Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)). On the web, that means `prefers-reduced-motion` for animated zoom-to-fit and sheet transitions. Whether Safari honours iOS Dynamic Type through `-apple-system-body` fonts was not verified and needs a device check.

## iOS 26 standalone Safari forces edge-to-edge layout with defensive insets

Since Safari 26, "every website added to the Home Screen opens as a web app" ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)). This makes the standalone shell the primary surface, not a niche one. Safari 26 tints its Liquid Glass chrome by sampling **fixed or sticky elements near the viewport edges** and **ignores `theme-color`**. Elements at `opacity:0` or with `pointer-events:none` are still sampled, and a transparent root background falls back to white. The fixes are explicit `html`/`body` backgrounds and `display:none` (or unmounting) for hidden sheets ([1ar.io](https://1ar.io/updates/safari-26-liquid-glass-web/)). An open dialog's background also recolours the status-bar area (WebKit bug 302272, via [Ben Frain](https://benfrain.com/ios26-safari-theme-color-tab-tinting-with-fixed-position-elements/)). A **reopened regression** means that since iOS 26.1, Home Screen apps cannot draw in the status-bar region. `env(safe-area-inset-top)` returns 0 there, yet about 62 px is unusable ([WebKit bug 301994](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=301994)). The repository's `status-bar-style=default` is the robust choice while that bug stays open. `black-translucent`, the only near-fullscreen option ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/ios-14.5/)), would put the top bar under the status bar.

Dynamic viewport units update with throttling ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/viewport-units)), so the editor root should use `100svh` (or a JS-measured `visualViewport.height`) to keep the bottom panel from jumping. Base UI's docs add `position: relative` on `body` for iOS 26 backdrops ([Base UI](https://base-ui.com/react/overview/quick-start)). The comparison canvas needs `touch-action: none` so the app's own gesture state machine (D-053) receives pinch and pan. The rest of the page keeps native zoom for accessibility. In browser mode, horizontal drags near the screen edge can trigger Safari's back-swipe, and no workaround reliably works in Mobile Safari ([WICG](https://discourse.WICG.io/t/proposal-api-to-control-user-gesture-navigation/1536)). The split grip and corner handles should therefore stay about 24 px from the screen edges. Handles that would sit closer are still grabbed by their nearest-handle hit radius. None of this geometry is documented by Apple, so it all needs real-iPhone verification (AGENTS rule 9).

### No global navigation until there are sections to navigate

Apple's tab-bar rules settle the navbar question. A tab bar is "to support navigation, not to provide actions," should stay visible except under a modal, and its buttons must not be hidden or disabled when content is unavailable. Instead, "if a section is empty, explain why" ([Apple HIG, Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). The current header links to sections that do not exist yet, which breaks the rule on unavailable content. For v0.1, the right structure is **no global navigation**. The start screen is the import screen, and the editor opens over it as a full-screen task. When Projekte and Coach actually ship, a three-tab bar with single-word German labels ("Vergleich", "Projekte", "Coach") and filled icons becomes appropriate, and the editor still covers it.

## Lucide and Base UI cover the stack under AGPL

**Icons: Lucide (`lucide-react`, ISC)** is the default. It has about 2,130 icons on a consistent 24 px / 2 px-stroke grid with per-icon ES imports. Phosphor (MIT, six weights including a fill weight), Tabler (MIT, 5,166 outline icons, including `perspective` and `border-corners`) and Material Symbols (Apache-2.0) are all license-compatible ([unpkg licenses](https://unpkg.com/lucide-static@latest/LICENSE); [npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/lucide-react/latest)). SF Symbols are **not licensed for web apps** ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/757407)). Lucide has no perspective or corner-pin glyph, so NextStroke should draw one custom "Ecken" icon on Lucide's grid rather than mix sets, because stroke weights and corner radii differ visibly between sets. Phosphor is the alternative if filled-when-selected icons become a convention, for example in a future tab bar.

**Headless components: Base UI (`@base-ui/react` 1.8.0, MIT)** ships Slider, Toggle Group, Toolbar (with roving focus), Dialog, Drawer (with swipe-to-dismiss and a `SwipeArea` part added in 1.3.0), Popover and Menu in one tree-shakable package. It is built by the creators of Radix, Floating UI and MUI, and its docs explicitly address iOS 26 Safari ([Base UI](https://base-ui.com/react/overview/quick-start); [Drawer](https://base-ui.com/react/components/drawer)). React Aria Components (Apache-2.0) is stronger on strict ARIA semantics but heavier. Radix's update velocity "has slowed for some complex components," and it needs Vaul for a drawer ([GreatFrontEnd](https://www.greatfrontend.com/blog/top-headless-ui-libraries-for-react-in-2026)). D-036 and spec §13.2 still name Radix, but `apps/web/package.json` currently depends on neither library, so switching costs nothing in code. Per AGENTS rule 8, the switch still requires a new decision entry and updates to the spec and plan. If the owner prefers not to reopen D-036, Radix plus Vaul is an acceptable fallback. The canvas itself (split divider, pinch, corner handles) stays in the app's own code per D-053. Base UI covers only the chrome.

## Design brief

### Principles

1. **The drawing is the interface.** The canvas gets at least 60% of the screen height in every mode, and at least 75% in Vergleich.
2. **One mode, one primary control.** Vergleich shows one slider. Ausrichten shows the layer and its handles. Ecken shows one image and four rings. Nothing else competes.
3. **Monochrome by default.** One accent colour, reserved for the selected state and for at most one prominent button per view. Toggles change shape or fill as well as colour.
4. **Standard symbols are icon-only, NextStroke concepts are labelled.** This follows the three tiers above. No label exceeds 12 characters.
5. **Every gesture has a visible twin.** Pinch, drag, rotate, hold and corner drag each have a tappable equivalent on screen.
6. **Nothing is destructive, so nothing asks "are you sure".** Originals are immutable and alignment is a reversible layer, so undo replaces confirmations. The one exception is leaving with unsaved alignment, which uses an action sheet.
7. **No dead ends.** Detection that fails still pre-places a quad. "No controlled result" is a stated outcome, never a blocked screen.
8. **Teach in place, once.** One hint strip per mode, then silence. Gestures are listed under Mehr → Gesten.

### Information architecture

v0.1 has three surfaces. **Start** (the import screen, no navigation bar) leads to **Editor** (full-screen, modes Vergleich / Ausrichten / Ecken). The **Export sheet** opens from the editor. The Ecken mode is a two-step sub-flow (Vorlage, then Zeichnung) inside the editor. Later releases add a three-tab bar (Vergleich, Projekte, Coach). In that structure, checkpoints live inside Projekte, coaching opens as a nonmodal panel in the editor, and AI stroke previews appear as a separate, labelled layer ("Experimentell – keine sichere Vorschau", product rule 6) that is never merged into the Vergleich slider.

The UI vocabulary is "Vorlage" for the reference (template or earlier state) and "Zeichnung" for the photo of the physical drawing (the "original" in the spec). Beginners read "Original" ambiguously, so the word stays out of labels.

### Start / import screen

The top of the screen shows the wordmark "NextStroke" (text, not a button) and a trailing "…" menu: Info, Datenschutz, Gesten. Below that sit two large stacked cards, each at least 120 px tall. The first card shows the photo icon, the title "Vorlage", the hint "Vorlage oder früherer Stand" and a text button "Bild wählen". The second card shows the camera icon, the title "Deine Zeichnung", the hint "Foto vom Papier – schräg ist okay" and the button "Foto wählen". Each card uses a plain `<input type="file" accept="image/*">` without `capture`, so iOS shows its own chooser for camera, library and files ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Attributes/capture)). The Vorlage card also accepts PDFs, using the existing page picker. A filled card shows a thumbnail with "Ändern". The one prominent button, "Vergleichen", sits at the bottom inside the thumb zone and stays inactive until both cards are filled. The empty state teaches the task in one line, "Zwei Bilder wählen, dann vergleichen", plus a quiet text link "Beispiel ansehen" that opens a bundled demo pair. On desktop each card also shows "oder Bild hierher ziehen". Features that are not available do not appear as links.

### Compare editor (Vergleich mode)

**Top bar** (on a solid backing plate, inside the safe area):
- Leading: back chevron.
- Centre group: undo and redo.
- Trailing: share icon and "…".

**Canvas** (fills the remaining space, `touch-action: none`):
- A floating zoom capsule sits at the bottom trailing edge, about 24 px inset.
- A transient pill ("Zeichnung" / "Vorlage") appears top-centre while the reveal is active.

**Bottom panel** (fixed, about 30% of the height):
- Row 1: the mode tabs, three icon+label buttons (Vergleich, Ausrichten, Ecken). Each shows a small dot when its setting differs from the default, borrowing Apple Photos' change ring.
- Row 2: the text segmented control "Überlagern | Teilen" on the leading side and the eye toggle on the trailing side.
- Row 3: the opacity slider, with a Vorlage thumbnail at the minimum end and a Zeichnung thumbnail at the maximum end. In Teilen it becomes the divider slider. Double-tap resets it to 50%.

In Teilen mode the canvas also shows the full-height divider with a 44 px grip and side labels ("Vorlage" / "Zeichnung").

### Ausrichten mode (move/scale/rotate the Vorlage layer)

The panel header row shows "Abbrechen" (text, leading), a centred title "Vorlage ausrichten", and the prominent "Fertig" (trailing). Below it the panel shows a non-interactive chip, "Gesten bewegen die Vorlage". The fine-adjust row ("Feinjustierung") follows: a four-arrow nudge pad, "Größe" with −/+ and a percentage readout, "Drehen" with ↺/↻ and a degree readout, and a "Fein/Grob" step toggle. A text button "Zurücksetzen" sits at the end. The opacity slider is still available, collapsed to a thin row, because people align by watching the overlay. Gestures in this mode are one-finger drag to move the layer, pinch to scale it (0.4–2×) and two-finger twist to rotate it.

### Ecken flow (four paper corners on both images)

**Step 1 of 2, "Ecken der Vorlage".** The panel header shows "Abbrechen", the step label "1/2 Vorlage" and "Weiter". The canvas shows only the Vorlage, zoomed out slightly so every ring is reachable. Four pre-placed rings are joined by the quad outline. They come from auto-detection once it exists, and until then from a default quad inset from the image edges.

The rings work as follows:
- Each ring is 16–20 px with a dual white/dark stroke and a crosshair centre. Its hit radius reaches about 60 px, and a press selects the nearest corner.
- Drags move the corner by delta.
- While a finger is down, a loupe of about 110 px appears at 3× (option 4×) with a crosshair, centred about 90–100 px above the touch. It flips below or to the side near the top edge.

The panel shows:
- A corner selector (four small buttons laid out like the paper, or tap a ring to select it).
- Nudge arrows that move the selected corner by one pixel per tap. The loupe stays visible while a corner is selected, so nudging is precise.
- The text buttons "Automatisch" (re-detect, shown once detection exists), "Ganzes Bild" (snap the quad to the image edges) and "Zurücksetzen".

If detection reports low confidence, a neutral note reads "Ecken prüfen". The user is never blocked.

**Step 2 of 2, "Ecken deiner Zeichnung".** This step is identical on the Zeichnung, with "Zurück" leading and the prominent "Fertig" trailing. Corner order is enforced with matching numbered labels (1–4) on both images, so the homography cannot pair crossed corners. On "Fertig" the editor returns to Vergleich with the rectified overlay at 50%. If the result fails the controlled-overlay bar, the canvas says so plainly ("Keine sichere Ausrichtung – manuell weiter ausrichten") instead of presenting it as aligned.

### Export sheet

A nonmodal half-height Base UI Drawer. It is unmounted when closed, because of the Safari 26 tinting issue. Its header shows "Exportieren" on the leading side and a close × on the trailing side. Three radio rows choose the content: "Aktuelle Ansicht", "Nur Zeichnung" and "Nur Vorlage". The prominent "Teilen" button uses the Web Share API inside the user gesture, and the text button "Bild sichern" downloads the file. The composite is rendered locally from the immutable original (product rule 4).

## Usability test plan with beginners

The test runs **three rounds of five participants**, fixing problems between rounds. Five users find about 85% of problems in Nielsen and Landauer's model. Quantitative claims would need about 20 users, so metrics from these rounds are directional only ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/)). Participants are adult hobby or beginner artists who own an iPhone. Each round includes at least one left-handed participant and one who uses enlarged text. Every session runs on a **real iPhone with the app installed to the Home Screen** (AGENTS rule 9), using the participant's own fineliner drawing in progress, or a supplied one, photographed at an angle on a table. No user artwork is kept after the session (AGENTS rule 9 on artwork).

| # | Task (read aloud, no UI words) | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Before any task, point to each top-bar and panel icon and say what it does | Icon named correctly (5-second rule) |
| 1 | "Load your drawing and this template so you can compare them." | Both images loaded and editor open |
| 2 | "Make the template see-through over your drawing and tell me where your lines differ." | Opacity changed; a correct difference named |
| 3 | "Quickly see your drawing without the template, then go back." | Reveal used (hold, tap or eye) |
| 4 | "Compare left and right halves instead." | Teilen chosen and divider moved |
| 5 | "Look closely at the eye area, then see the whole page again." | Zoom in and fit, by any means |
| 6 | "The photo is tilted. Make the paper line up with the template." | Both corner steps completed, overlay aligned within tolerance |
| 7 | "Move this one corner a tiny bit without dragging it." | Selector plus arrows used |
| 8 | "You moved the template by mistake. Undo it." | Undo or Abbrechen used |
| 9 | "Save a picture of this comparison to your Photos." | Exported or shared image exists |
| 10 | "Swap in a different photo of your drawing." | Image replaced without starting over |

**Measures per task:**
- Binary or partial success.
- Time on task.
- Wrong paths and errors (wrong mode, accidental tap toggles, Safari back-swipe, accidental pinch of the page).
- Whether a gesture was **discovered unaided** or only through its visible twin.
- Hint dismissal without reading.
- The **SEQ** after each task, a 7-point scale where the typical average is about 5.5 ([MeasuringU](https://measuringu.com/seq10/)).

Task 0 gives icon comprehension per glyph. SUS at the end gives only a coarse benchmark. A German SEQ/SUS wording was not validated and needs to be picked before round 1. Two triggers prompt a redesign of a control: failure by two or more of five participants, or an SEQ below about 5.5. Those triggers are a working heuristic, not a sourced threshold. In particular, any tier-1 icon misread by two participants moves to tier 2 (icon plus label). Separately from the beginner rounds, the team runs one VoiceOver pass and one 200%-text pass on the same device.

## Conclusion

The research turns the owner's verdict into specific, checkable defects. The problem is not too few features but too many controls at equal weight, none of them anchored to a mode. The fix borrows the photo-editor skeleton wholesale, the scanner corner mechanics almost wholesale, and GitHub's reasoning about compare modes. What NextStroke adds is the two-image corner pairing that no art app offers, so that flow, not the button styling, is where the usability rounds should spend their scrutiny.

Three decisions remain open, and testing should settle them rather than taste. First, whether beginners understand an outline eye and a share glyph without labels. Second, whether a canvas tap that toggles the drawing causes more accidental state changes than it saves taps. Third, whether a 3× loupe is enough for paper corners photographed at a steep angle. Two administrative steps also come before code: a decision entry that replaces Radix with Base UI (or confirms Radix plus Vaul), and a real-device check of the iOS 26 status-bar regression and Wake Lock support, since both directly shape the top bar and the drawing-session features.
